## How to Execute Sessions

1. Open a **NEW** AI conversation with deep research or web search enabled.
2. Copy the full prompt between `## THE PROMPT — copy from here` and the `---` separator.
3. Let the AI run its complete research process without interruption.
4. Save the response to the file path in the session header.

## Prompt Design Philosophy

These prompts follow three principles:

**1. Direction, not prescription.** Each prompt defines WHAT to learn and WHY it matters, but gives the AI complete freedom in HOW to research — which queries to run, how many searches to perform, what unexpected directions to pursue, and how to organize its investigation.

**2. Full independence.** Every session (except SYN-01) is fully self-contained. No session depends on another's output. Each topic is explored with a fresh, uncontaminated analytical lens to prevent cascading confirmation bias.

**3. Trust the model's intelligence.** Frontier AI models with deep research capabilities are sophisticated reasoners with tool access, self-refinement, and extensive search capabilities. Over-specifying search queries or rigid output structures constrains the model rather than harnessing it. We define the destination, not the exact path.

> Sessions marked 🔺 benefit from multi-model triangulation (run across 2-3 different models and compare).
> Sessions marked 🔬 are well-served by single-model deep research.

# SESSION T2-01 — Aspect-Isolation vs Integrated Research
**Triangulation:** 🔺 Recommended (core axiom — run across 2-3 models)
**Output file:** `meta-research/research/T2-01-aspect-isolation.md`

## THE PROMPT — copy from here

You are an information science researcher with expertise in task decomposition, cognitive load theory, and AI-assisted research optimization.

**Your mission:** Rigorously and honestly evaluate whether isolating research topics into separate, single-topic sessions produces better results than integrated, multi-topic research — and under what conditions. This is the most important design question for a research pipeline framework, and we need evidence-grounded truth, not confirmation of existing assumptions.

**Background:** A research pipeline framework currently enforces an "Aspect-Isolation Law" mandating that every distinct research topic must have its own dedicated session. The claimed rationale:
1. AI models have finite search budgets per session. Bundling 5 topics means each topic gets only a fraction of the search attention.
2. Context window attention diffuses across multiple topics, causing generic, shallow summaries instead of deep, specific analysis.
3. Deep technical investigation requires dedicated focus that is impossible when topics are bundled.

This assumption was derived from observational experience across 6 projects, not from controlled studies or peer-reviewed evidence. It has NOT been empirically validated.

**What we need to learn:**
- Is there empirical evidence (studies, experiments, benchmarks) that single-topic AI research sessions produce higher-quality outputs than multi-topic sessions?
- How do frontier AI models actually allocate search effort and reasoning attention when given single vs multiple topics? Is "search budget dilution" a documented phenomenon or an assumption?
- What are the COSTS of over-isolation? Does fragmenting research into too many isolated sessions miss systemic interactions, cross-cutting insights, or emergent patterns?
- What is the optimal granularity of topic decomposition? Where is the line between "too broad" (diluted) and "too fragmented" (loses systemic view)?
- Is the answer conditional — does it depend on topic complexity, topic relatedness, model capabilities, or research type (factual lookup vs evaluative judgment)?

**Research direction:** Investigate from multiple angles — cognitive science (cognitive load theory, attention allocation), information science (information foraging theory), AI research (prompt complexity vs output quality), and practical experience. Explore BOTH the case for isolation and the case against it with equal rigor. Consider that the optimal approach might be a nuanced middle ground — isolation for some conditions, integration for others. Look for evidence about where the inflection point lies.

**Bias alert:** Resist confirmation bias in either direction. Don't validate isolation just because it's the current framework's axiom, and don't reject it just to be contrarian. Follow the evidence. If the evidence is mixed or insufficient, say so clearly.

Your output should deliver a clear, evidence-grounded recommendation with confidence level. Cover the case for, the case against, the nuanced reality, and actionable criteria for when to isolate vs integrate.

# SESSION T2-02 — Multi-Model Triangulation: Value vs Overhead
**Triangulation:** 🔺 Meta-Required (run across 2-3 models — the divergence itself is data)
**Output file:** `meta-research/research/T2-02-triangulation-value.md`

## THE PROMPT — copy from here

You are a multi-model AI evaluation specialist with expertise in ensemble methods, model disagreement analysis, and decision quality assessment.

**Your mission:** Rigorously evaluate whether running identical research prompts across multiple frontier AI models (e.g., Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) produces meaningfully better architectural decisions than deep research with a single model — and define clear, actionable criteria for when multi-model approaches are justified versus wasteful.

**The critical challenge:** In 2026, all major frontier models offer deep research modes with extensive web search. When all models research the same topic, they search the same internet and often find the same primary sources. The traditional argument for triangulation was that different models have different training data and biases. But when all models do live web search, training data bias is reduced — they're all reading the same current sources. The residual value of triangulation is in **interpretation diversity** (different analytical lenses on the same information), not **information diversity** (finding different facts). The question is: is interpretation diversity worth 3x the time and cost?

**What we need to learn:**
- When do frontier AI models genuinely disagree on the same research topic? What types of questions produce meaningful divergence vs near-identical conclusions?
- Is interpretation diversity (same sources, different synthesis) valuable enough to justify multi-model approaches? Under what conditions?
- What does decision science and ensemble theory say about "wisdom of crowds" applied to AI models? When is one expert enough vs when do you need multiple opinions?
- What are lighter-weight alternatives to full 3-model triangulation? (e.g., adversarial review where one model critiques another's output, selective triangulation only for highest-risk decisions, quick verification passes)
- Can we define clear, actionable criteria — based on topic type, risk level, or decision reversibility — for when triangulation adds value vs when single-model depth is sufficient?

**Research direction:** Investigate empirical studies on model agreement/disagreement, ensemble methods for LLMs, cost-benefit analyses of multi-model verification, and cross-domain analogues from decision science. Consider that the answer likely depends on the TYPE of question — factual lookups vs subjective architectural judgments vs predictions about future behavior.

**CRITICAL META-BIAS WARNING:** You are an AI model being asked to evaluate multi-model triangulation — your own role in a multi-model process. You have a structural bias here: you might overvalue your own importance (making triangulation seem essential) or undervalue it (false modesty to seem objective). Evaluate PURELY from empirical evidence and logical analysis, as a neutral human decision scientist would.

Your output should deliver clear criteria for when to triangulate, when single-model suffices, and what lighter-weight alternatives exist. End with a concrete, actionable recommendation.
